How Car Insurance Rates Are Calculated

Auto insurance underwriters evaluate risk using actuarial tables that estimate the likelihood of a claim. The most influential variables include:

  • Driving Record & Claims History: An at-fault accident raises premiums by an average of 44%, while a moving violation (such as speeding) adds roughly 22% for 36 months.
  • Vehicle Class & Repair Complexity: Modern electric vehicles and luxury performance cars cost more to insure because battery containment, LiDAR sensors, and bumper cameras require specialized technician labor.
  • Credit-Based Insurance Score: In states where permitted (all except CA, HI, MA, and MI), drivers with excellent credit pay up to 40% less than drivers with poor credit for identical coverage.
  • Selected Deductibles: Choosing a $1,000 collision deductible rather than $500 saves approximately 14% on your comprehensive and collision bill.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between state minimum and full coverage?

State minimum liability only pays for injuries and property damage you cause to other people. It does not pay a single dollar to fix your own car. Full coverage adds Collision (damage from accidents) and Comprehensive (theft, hail, vandalism, windshield damage).

How can I instantly lower my auto premium?

Ask your insurer about paperless auto-pay discounts, multi-car discounts, defensive driving certificates, and safe-driver telematics programs like Progressive Snapshot or State Farm Drive Safe & Save.
