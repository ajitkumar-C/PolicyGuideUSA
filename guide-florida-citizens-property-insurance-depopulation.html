1. The Florida Property Crisis: Why Citizens Is Shedding 300,000+ Policies

Citizens Property Insurance Corporation was created by the Florida Legislature as the state’s insurer of last resort—a safety net designed exclusively for property owners unable to obtain coverage in the voluntary private market. However, following the insolvencies of more than a dozen private Florida insurers between 2021 and 2024, Citizens ballooned to over 1.4 million active policies, representing hundreds of billions of dollars in catastrophic hurricane exposure.

To prevent a catastrophic statewide financial collapse, the Florida Legislature enacted major statutory reforms (Senate Bills 2A and 1028) aimed at "depopulating" Citizens. Through this program, authorized private insurance companies (such as Slide, Florida Peninsula, TypTap, and Edison) review Citizens policy files and submit requests to "take out" or assume policies back into the private sector.

2. The 20% Takeout Rule Explained (With Real Dollar Math)

Prior to recent legislative reforms, Florida homeowners could arbitrarily reject any private takeout offer and choose to stay with Citizens indefinitely. Today, that option is statutorily restricted by the 20% Price Differential Threshold:

$3,800 / year $4,200 / year $4,800 / year +14.3% (Under 20%) Mandatory Exit: Must accept private offer or find another carrier. Cannot stay with Citizens.
$3,800 / year $4,200 / year $5,250 / year +25.0% (Over 20%) Optional Choice: You may reject the takeout offer and renew with Citizens.
$4,500 / year $5,000 / year $4,900 / year -2.0% (Lower Cost) Mandatory Exit: Offer is cheaper than Citizens renewal. Policy is assumed automatically.

If you receive an offer from an authorized private carrier that is less than 20% higher than your Citizens renewal rate, your policy will be transferred automatically unless you procure an entirely different policy from an alternative admitted or surplus carrier.

3. The Florida Citizens Mandatory Flood Insurance Timeline

Historically, standard homeowners insurance policies (including Citizens HO-3) exclude all water damage caused by storm surge, tidal overflow, and surface runoff. Under Florida Statute § 627.351(6), Citizens now mandates that every personal residential policyholder must carry flood insurance, even if your property is on high ground in FEMA Flood Zone X:

  • Homes in High-Risk Special Flood Hazard Areas (A & V Zones): Mandatory flood coverage took effect immediately for all Coverage A structures.
  • Structures with Dwelling Value ≥ $600,000: Mandatory flood requirement took effect January 1, 2024.
  • Structures with Dwelling Value ≥ $500,000: Mandatory flood requirement took effect January 1, 2025.
  • Structures with Dwelling Value ≥ $400,000: Mandatory flood requirement took effect January 1, 2026.
  • All Remaining Personal Residential Policies: Universal flood coverage required by January 1, 2027, regardless of structure value or FEMA zone.

Failing to purchase flood insurance through either the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) or an approved private flood insurer results in immediate statutory cancellation of your Citizens wind and fire policy.

4. How to Vet Private Takeout Carriers: Demotech, KBRA & Surplus Lines

Many homeowners worry that private takeout carriers lack the balance sheet depth to survive a Category 4 or 5 hurricane hitting Miami or Tampa. When evaluating a takeout carrier, focus on these financial indicators:

  • Financial Stability Rating: Verify whether the insurer holds an A (Exceptional) rating from Demotech, or an investment-grade rating from Kroll Bond Rating Agency (KBRA) or AM Best. Mortgage servicers (Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac) require approved ratings to honor your loan escrow.
  • Florida Insurance Guaranty Association (FIGA): Ensure the carrier is an admitted Florida insurer. If an admitted carrier enters insolvency after a hurricane, FIGA pays covered claims up to $300,000 (or $500,000 for primary residential structures). Non-admitted surplus lines carriers do NOT have FIGA backing.
  • Reinsurance Treaty Structure: Inquire with your insurance agent regarding the carrier's reinsurance attachment point and catastrophic treaty coverage through the Florida Hurricane Catastrophe Fund (FHCF).

5. 4-Step Action Checklist When You Receive a Depopulation Letter

  1. Check the Postmark and Response Deadline: Citizens depopulation letters typically give policyholders only 30 days to respond. Missing this deadline triggers automatic assumption by the private insurer.
  2. Compare the Coverage Line-by-Line: Ensure the takeout carrier provides identical replacement cost limits (Coverage A), personal property (Coverage C), and loss of use (Coverage D). Watch out for cosmetic roof damage exclusions and water loss sub-limits ($10,000 caps).
  3. Calculate Your Combined Flood + Home Dues: Add the cost of mandatory NFIP flood coverage to your takeout rate to get an accurate total housing expense projection.
  4. Confirm With Your Independent Agent: Independent agents can check if another admitted carrier in the open market offers lower premiums or superior windstorm deductible terms before accepting the takeout carrier.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Citizens 20% depopulation rule in Florida?

Under Florida law, if an authorized private insurance company offers to assume your policy and their premium is within 20% of your Citizens renewal quote, you are statutorily ineligible to remain with Citizens and must accept the private offer or find another carrier.

Does Citizens Property Insurance require flood insurance in Florida?

Yes. Florida law requires all Citizens personal residential policyholders to maintain continuous flood insurance. By 2027, every single Citizens policyholder—regardless of flood zone or elevation—must have an active flood policy to maintain coverage.

What happens if I ignore a Citizens takeout notice letter?

If you do not register a choice or if the private offer is within the 20% statutory threshold, your policy is automatically assumed by the private takeout insurer on the assumption date specified in the notice.
