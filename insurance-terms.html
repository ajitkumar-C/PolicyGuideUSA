🏛️ Level 0: What is Property & Casualty Insurance?

First-Party vs. Third-Party

Before diving into individual policy forms, every beginner must understand the fundamental divide that defines P&C: Property Insurance protects your own assets, whereas Casualty (Liability) Insurance protects your financial liability to others.

First-Party Coverage

Property Insurance

Covers physical loss or damage to property that you own, lease, or occupy. The insurance payout goes directly to you (the first party) to repair or replace the asset.

  • Core Perils: Fire, Lightning, Windstorm, Hail, Theft, Explosion.
  • Valuation Basis: Actual Cash Value (ACV) or Replacement Cost (RCV).
  • Common Examples: Homeowners (HO-3), Auto Comprehensive & Collision, Commercial Building & Personal Property.
Third-Party Coverage

Casualty & Liability Insurance

Protects you against legal liability for unintentional bodily injury or property damage inflicted upon a third party. The insurer defends you in court and pays settlements directly to the claimant.

  • Triggers: Tort liability, common-law negligence, statutory obligations.
  • Key Inclusions: Legal defense costs (outside or inside policy limits).
  • Common Examples: Auto Bodily Injury Liability, Commercial General Liability (CGL), Professional Malpractice, Workers' Comp Employer's Liability.

⚖️ Fundamental Insurance Doctrines & Principles

Universal Concepts

These principles form the legal and economic bedrock of all property, casualty, and commercial contracts across all 50 states.

Risk

Doctrine
The measurable uncertainty concerning the occurrence or severity of a financial loss.
In Plain English: In insurance, risk is the chance that something bad happens causing financial damage. Insurers only insure pure risks (where there is only chance of loss or no loss, like a house fire) — never speculative risks (like gambling or stock trading where you might gain a profit).
💡 Real-World Scenario
Buying a home in a coastal county introduces the pure risk of windstorm damage. You cannot profit from the storm, only protect against the loss.
Pure vs. Speculative Rate Risk Factors →

Peril

Doctrine
The specific, proximate event or cause that gives rise to a loss.
In Plain English: Peril is the "what happened." It is the destructive force that triggered the damage. Common perils include fire, hail, theft, lightning, freezing pipes, or a vehicle collision.
💡 Real-World Scenario
If lightning strikes an oak tree that falls through your roof, lightning is the proximate peril that triggered the physical damage.
Named vs. Open Perils Home Peril Estimator →

Hazard

Doctrine
A physical or behavioral condition that increases the likelihood or severity of a loss from a peril.
In Plain English: While peril is the cause of loss, a hazard is a condition that makes the loss more likely. There are 3 types:
• Physical: Frayed wiring, icy road.
• Moral: Dishonesty or fraud (arson).
• Morale (Attitudinal): Carelessness ("Why lock my car? It's insured").
💡 Real-World Scenario
Leaving dead tree limbs hanging over your garage is a physical hazard that increases the risk of damage during a windstorm peril.
Underwriting Factor Audit Risk Level →

Principle of Indemnity

Legal Principle
The fundamental insurance doctrine stating that coverage restores the insured to their exact pre-loss financial position without allowing unjust enrichment or profit.
In Plain English: You cannot make a profit from an insurance claim. If your 7-year-old couch is destroyed in a fire, an indemnity policy pays the actual cash value or replacement cost to restore what you had, not enough to upgrade you to luxury furniture for profit.
💡 Real-World Scenario
Insuring a $20,000 used car with three different carriers does not allow you to collect $60,000 if it is totaled; insurers coordinate benefits to strictly pay $20,000.
Statutory Rule ACV vs RCV Deep Dive →

Subrogation

Legal Right
The legal assignment of rights allowing an insurer that paid a claim to pursue the legally liable third party to recover the payout.
In Plain English: When someone else causes you damage, your insurance company can pay you immediately to fix your car or home, and then "step into your shoes" to legally demand repayment from the at-fault driver's insurance company.
💡 Real-World Scenario
Another driver rear-ends you at a red light. Your carrier pays $8,000 to fix your car, waives or recovers your $500 deductible, and subrogates against the at-fault driver's carrier to recoup the full $8,500.
Post-Claim Recovery Claims Guide →

Insurable Interest

Legal Requirement
A legal or financial stake in the preservation of an asset or person, such that its destruction would cause the policyholder direct monetary loss.
In Plain English: You cannot purchase insurance on your neighbor's house or stranger's car hoping it gets damaged so you can collect money. In P&C insurance, insurable interest must exist at the time of the loss.
💡 Real-World Scenario
Both a homeowner and their mortgage lender hold an insurable interest in the property, which is why mortgage companies are listed as "Loss Payees" on the policy.

🚗 Motor Insurance (Personal & Commercial)

Slide 8 - Branch 1

Motor lines protect passenger vehicles, rideshare drivers, and commercial fleets against vehicular collisions, comprehensive physical perils, and multi-state statutory liability mandates.

Deductible / Excess

Motor & Property
The predetermined dollar amount or percentage that the insured must bear out-of-pocket before insurance indemnification takes effect.
In Plain English: In the US it is called a deductible; in the UK and commonwealth nations it is called an excess. If repairs cost $4,000 and your deductible is $500, you pay $500 to the repair shop, and the insurer pays the remaining $3,500.
Payout = Min(Claim Amount - Deductible, Policy Limit)
Cost Lever Run Deductible Simulator →

Split Limits

Motor Liability
Liability coverage expressed as three numbers indicating maximum dollar caps for: (1) Bodily injury per person, (2) Bodily injury per accident, and (3) Property damage per accident.
In Plain English: If your auto policy states 100/300/100, it will pay up to:
• $100,000 medical costs for any one injured victim.
• $300,000 total for all injured victims combined in that accident.
• $100,000 for property damage (cars, guardrails, buildings).
💡 Real-World Scenario
If you cause an accident injuring 3 passengers for $80k, $90k, and $150k, the third passenger receives only the $100k cap, leaving you personally exposed to the remaining $50k.
Auto Liability Test Auto Limits →

Collision vs. Comprehensive (OTC)

Physical Damage
Two distinct physical damage coverages: Collision covers contact with another vehicle or stationary object; Comprehensive covers non-collision events (theft, fire, weather, animal strike).
In Plain English:
• Collision: You hit another car, a tree, a telephone pole, or flip over.
• Comprehensive: A deer jumps in front of you, a hailstorm dents your hood, a tree branch falls, or your vehicle is stolen.
💡 Real-World Scenario
Striking a live deer is classified as Comprehensive (unpredictable animal peril), but swerving to avoid the deer and hitting a guardrail is classified as Collision.
Full Coverage Full Comparison Guide →

Personal Injury Protection (PIP)

No-Fault Medical
A first-party, no-fault coverage required in many states that reimburses medical expenses, rehabilitation, and lost wages regardless of who caused the accident.
In Plain English: In "no-fault" states (like Florida, New York, or Michigan), your own auto insurance pays your initial hospital bills and lost income through PIP, avoiding delay from arguing who was to blame.
Statutory No-Fault Check State Rules →

UM / UIM Coverage

Protection Against Others
Uninsured Motorist (UM) and Underinsured Motorist (UIM) cover medical bills and property damage when an at-fault driver has zero insurance or insufficient limits to cover your loss.
In Plain English: Approximately 14% of US drivers carry no insurance. If an uninsured driver hits you, your UM policy steps in as if they carried adequate insurance.
Crucial Safeguard UM/UIM Guide →

Commercial Auto & HNOA

Commercial Motor
Business Auto Policy (BAP) insuring commercial vehicles, trucks, and fleets. Hired and Non-Owned Auto (HNOA) extends liability to employee personal cars used for business errands.
In Plain English: Personal auto policies strictly exclude business deliveries. If an employee uses their personal car to pick up catering or drive between clients and causes a crash, the business needs HNOA to avoid being sued.

🏡 Property Insurance (Personal & Commercial)

Slide 8 - Branch 2

Property lines insure physical buildings, roofs, personal belongings, inventory, and equipment against direct perils such as fire, lightning, wind, and hail.

ACV vs. Replacement Cost (RCV)

Valuation Method
Actual Cash Value (ACV) pays replacement cost minus depreciation; Replacement Cost Value (RCV) pays the full current cost to rebuild or replace with new materials of like kind and quality.
In Plain English: If your 15-year-old roof was destroyed, an ACV policy pays only what a 15-year-old roof is worth today (substantially reduced by depreciation). An RCV policy pays the full invoice cost for a brand-new roof today.
ACV = Replacement Cost - Depreciation
Claim Payout Method Compare ACV vs RCV →

Coinsurance Clause (80% Rule)

Underwriting Requirement
A policy provision requiring the insured to maintain coverage equal to a minimum percentage (typically 80% or 90%) of the property's total value, penalizing partial loss payouts if unmet.
In Plain English: If your building is worth $1,000,000 and requires 80% coverage ($800,000), but you only bought $400,000 of coverage, you only carried 50% of the required insurance. Any partial claim payout will be reduced by 50%!
Payout = (Did Insure / Should Insure) × Loss - Deductible

Homeowners Policy Forms (HO-3, HO-4, HO-6)

Personal Property
Standardized ISO policy forms: HO-3 is the standard homeowner special form; HO-4 is renters contents insurance; HO-6 covers condominium unit interiors.
In Plain English:
• HO-3: Protects single-family homes (Open perils for structure, named perils for contents).
• HO-4: For tenants, covering furniture, electronics, and liability (no building structure).
• HO-6: "Walls-in" coverage for condo owners (cabinets, floors, improvements).
Personal Lines Renters Calculator →

Building & Personal Property (BPP)

Commercial Property
The core commercial property form insuring non-residential structures, permanently installed fixtures, machinery, raw inventory, and tenant improvements.
In Plain English: Covers the warehouse, storefront, or office building plus everything inside used to conduct business — desks, computers, machinery, and warehouse stock waiting for delivery.
Commercial Assets BOP & Property Tool →

Open Perils vs. Named Perils

Coverage Breadth
Named Perils only covers specifically listed events (e.g. fire, hail). Open Perils (All-Risk) covers every direct physical loss unless explicitly excluded in the policy contract.
In Plain English: In Open Perils, if an unusual event happens (like a wild animal smashing through a window) and the contract doesn't explicitly exclude it, it is covered. The burden of proof is on the insurance carrier to prove an exclusion applies.
ISO Standard Exclusions Guide →

Exclusions: Flood & Earth Movement

Contract Limitations
Universal property exclusions that require separate standalone policies (e.g. NFIP federal flood insurance or CEA earthquake policies).
In Plain English: Standard home and commercial policies never cover rising floodwaters from outdoors or seismic tremors from earthquakes. Separate specialized policies or riders are legally required.

⚖️ Liability & Casualty Insurance

Slide 8 - Branch 3

Casualty lines protect individuals and commercial enterprises against third-party lawsuits alleging negligence, bodily injury, workplace accidents, professional malpractice, and defective products.

Commercial General Liability (CGL)

Casualty Foundation
Comprehensive liability coverage defending a business against lawsuits alleging third-party bodily injury, property damage, and personal & advertising injury.
In Plain English: The foundational liability policy for any business. It pays attorney fees, court costs, and settlements if a customer slips and falls on a wet floor in your store or an employee drops a ladder onto a customer's car.
Business Must-Have Full CGL Guide →

Employer's Liability (WC Part 2)

Workers' Comp
Part Two of a standard Workers' Compensation policy covering common-law tort claims brought by employees against employers outside the statutory compensation system.
In Plain English: While standard workers' comp (Part 1) automatically pays medical bills for on-the-job injuries, Employer's Liability (Part 2) protects the employer if a worker's family sues for gross negligence or loss of consortium.
Statutory Immunity Compliance Audit →

Professional Liability (E&O)

Errors & Omissions
Liability coverage for financial losses caused by professional malpractice, mistakes, bad advice, failure to deliver services, or design oversights.
In Plain English: General liability only covers physical injury or property damage. If an accountant, lawyer, software engineer, or insurance broker gives bad advice that costs a client $200,000, only an E&O policy covers the lawsuit.
Service Professionals Business Liability →

Product Liability

Strict Liability
Covers liability arising from physical injury or property damage caused by a defect in products manufactured, distributed, or sold by the business.
In Plain English: If an electronic appliance overheats and burns down a customer's house, or a batch of food causes illness, the manufacturer, distributor, and retailer face strict legal liability. Product liability pays legal defense and judgments.
Supply Chain Risk Carrier Rankings →

Occurrence vs. Claims-Made Triggers

Coverage Trigger
Occurrence policies respond if the injury occurred during the policy term (regardless of when reported). Claims-Made policies require the claim to be filed while the policy is active and after the retroactive date.
In Plain English: An Occurrence policy purchased in 2024 protects you forever if someone was injured in 2024, even if they sue in 2028. A Claims-Made policy only protects you if the claim is officially reported while the policy remains active.
Policy Mechanics Trigger Details →

Duty to Defend vs. Indemnify

Legal Obligation
The insurer's contractual obligation to provide legal counsel and pay litigation costs, which is broader than the obligation to pay final damage judgments (indemnification).
In Plain English: Even if a plaintiff files a frivolous lawsuit against you, your liability insurer must hire attorneys and pay for your legal defense as long as any part of the lawsuit could potentially fall under coverage.

🌐 Specialty & Financial Lines ("Others")

Slide 8 - Branch 4

Encompasses unique, high-severity, or mobile risks that fall outside standard static property and liability forms: marine cargo, aviation, international travel, and business revenue protection.

Marine Insurance (Ocean & Inland)

Specialty Lines
Ocean Marine covers vessels (Hull), cargo, shipping freight, and maritime liability (Protection & Indemnity). Inland Marine covers domestic goods in transit and mobile high-value equipment.
In Plain English: Marine insurance was the historical origin of modern insurance in London coffee houses. Today, Ocean Marine covers cargo ships and containers on the sea, while Inland Marine protects high-value contractor equipment, cameras, and jewelry that move from location to location.
Transit & Watercraft Watercraft Calculator →

Aircraft / Aviation Insurance

Aviation Lines
Covers physical damage to aircraft hulls (in-flight or ground), passenger liability, third-party property damage, and airport hangarkeepers legal liability.
In Plain English: Airplanes and helicopters carry catastrophic liability potential. Aviation insurance separates physical aircraft damage (Hull) from high-limit passenger liability and ground damage to airport hangars.
Aviation Risk Commercial Tools →

Travel Insurance

Consumer Specialty
Protects consumers against non-refundable trip cancellations, travel delays, lost luggage, emergency medical care abroad, and medical evacuation.
In Plain English: US domestic health insurance (and Medicare) rarely pays medical bills outside the country. Travel insurance reimburses emergency airlift evacuations and non-refundable flights if severe illness strikes before departure.
Personal Transit Travel Calculator →

Business Interruption (BI)

Commercial Income
Replaces lost operating income and covers ongoing fixed expenses (payroll, rent, taxes) while business premises are being repaired following direct physical damage from a covered peril.
In Plain English: If a fire guts your restaurant, property insurance rebuilds the kitchen, but Business Interruption insurance pays your chef's salary, restaurant rent, and expected net profit while the doors are closed.
💡 Real-World Scenario
Features a Period of Restoration (typically 12 to 24 months) and a Waiting Period (typically 48 or 72 hours) acting as a deductible before payments start.
Revenue Safeguard BOP & BI Guide →

Contingent Business Interruption (CBI)

Supply Chain
Extends business income coverage to losses resulting from physical property damage to the premises of key suppliers or customers that your business depends on.
In Plain English: Your factory experienced no fire or damage, but your sole parts supplier's facility burned down in another state, shutting down your assembly line. CBI covers your lost revenue.

📝 Underwriting, Policy Anatomy & Premium Accounting

Carrier Operations

How insurance companies underwrite risks, price contracts, structure legal binders, and account for premium revenue on statutory balance sheets.

Exposure Unit

Underwriting Unit
The standardized unit of measure used by underwriters and actuaries to calculate the premium for a given risk exposure.
In Plain English: Insurance rates are always priced per unit.
• For Auto: Per vehicle / per year.
• For Workers' Comp: Per $100 of gross payroll.
• For Property: Per $1,000 of building value.
• For CGL: Per $1,000 of gross sales or per 1,000 sq ft.
Premium = Exposure Units × Premium Rate
Pricing Base Vehicle Rate Estimator →

Written Premium (GWP)

Premium Accounting
The total premium contractual amount billed or collected on policies issued during an accounting period, regardless of policy term duration.
In Plain English: If an insurer sells 1,000 auto policies today at $1,200 each, their Written Premium today is $1,200,000. However, they have not yet earned that money because they still have 365 days of future coverage to provide.
Carrier Financials Compare Carrier Size →

Earned Premium vs. Unearned (UPR)

Statutory Accounting
Earned Premium represents the portion of premium recognized as income as policy time elapses; Unearned Premium is held as a balance sheet liability for the unexpired policy period.
In Plain English: If you pay $1,200 upfront for a 12-month auto policy, the carrier earns $100 each month that passes without cancellation. After 3 months, $300 is Earned Premium and $900 remains in the Unearned Premium Reserve. If you cancel, the carrier must refund the unearned $900.
Earned Premium = Total Premium × (Elapsed Days / 365)
Financial Metric Carrier Stability →

Declarations Page ("Dec Page")

Policy Anatomy
The front-page summary of an insurance contract listing the named insured, policy term dates, covered property/vehicles, coverage limits, deductibles, and premium breakdown.
In Plain English: The "cheat sheet" of your insurance policy. When you rent an apartment or buy a car, landlords and lenders ask for your Dec Page because it proves exactly who is insured, how much coverage exists, and what your deductibles are in one single document.
Essential Document Switching Guide →

Endorsements & Riders

Contract Amendment
Written contractual amendments attached to an insurance policy that expand, restrict, or modify the terms of the original master policy form.
In Plain English: Standard policies have boilerplate text. An endorsement is a specialized add-on that changes the contract — like adding a jewelry floater, adding water backup coverage, or adding a teen driver to your auto policy.
Custom Coverage Riders Guide →

Insurance Binder

Temporary Contract
A temporary legal contract issued by an agent or insurer providing immediate, binding insurance coverage until the permanent formal policy is processed and issued.
In Plain English: When you buy a house or drive a new car off the dealership lot, the formal 60-page policy takes weeks to issue. The agent gives you a 1-page Binder, which legally guarantees coverage to your mortgage lender or auto lender right then and there.

📈 Claims Management & Actuarial Ratios

Slide 1 & Actuarial Math

The operational engine of an insurance carrier: from the First Notice of Loss (FNOL) to loss reserves, subrogation recovery, and NAIC financial solvency ratios.

First Notice of Loss (FNOL)

Slide 1 Focus
The initial formal notification given by an insured to the insurance company reporting that a loss, collision, or incident has occurred.
In Plain English: The very first step in the claims process. When you dial your carrier's hotline or open their mobile app after an accident and provide details (date, time, location, photos), you are officially filing an FNOL. It generates a claim number and assigns an adjuster.
Claim Step 1 Accident Checklist →

Loss Ratio

Slide 1 Focus
The fundamental profitability metric measuring the percentage of earned premiums paid out in claims and loss adjustment expenses (LAE).
In Plain English: If an insurer collects $100M in earned premium and pays out $65M in claims and legal defense costs, its loss ratio is 65%. A loss ratio over 75% indicates underwriting distress or severe catastrophic weather losses.
Loss Ratio = (Claims Paid + LAE) / Earned Premium
Carrier Health Compare Carrier Payouts →

Combined Ratio

Master Solvency Ratio
The ultimate measure of an insurance company's underwriting profitability, calculated as the sum of the Loss Ratio and Expense Ratio.
In Plain English:
• Combined Ratio < 100%: The company made an underwriting profit (e.g. 94% means $0.06 profit per dollar).
• Combined Ratio > 100%: The company lost money on underwriting and relies on investment returns on its float to survive.
Combined Ratio = Loss Ratio + Expense Ratio
Wall Street Metric Carrier Balance Sheets →

Loss Reserves & IBNR

Actuarial Reserves
Estimated funds set aside on an insurer's balance sheet to pay for reported claims currently being processed, plus an actuarial estimate for losses Incurred But Not Reported (IBNR).
In Plain English: When a hurricane hits on December 30th, thousands of claims won't be filed until January or February. State insurance regulators require carriers to maintain strict cash reserves (IBNR) to guarantee money is waiting for those late-reported claims.
Solvency Protection State Regulators →

Claims Adjuster

Claims Role
A licensed insurance professional who inspects property damage, interviews witnesses, interprets policy coverages, and negotiates claim settlement payouts.
In Plain English:
• Staff Adjuster: Full-time employee of the insurance company.
• Independent Adjuster (IA): Hired by insurers during catastrophes (hurricanes/wildfires).
• Public Adjuster (PA): Hired by you (the policyholder) to represent you against the insurance company.
